Product design challenge · Home + Add items redesign

Open Alta.
Tap once.
Get dressed.

Alta loses people on the busy mornings it was built for. This redesign turns Home into a one-tap decision about today and turns adding clothes into a review you can finish in seconds. Both screens feed the same habit: come back next week and pick an outfit.

The problem

Slower than the closet

Alta has to beat the ten seconds it takes to grab a usual outfit. Today it asks for more: scroll past friends' posts, find suggestions below the fold, dig a saved look out of a calendar. I ranked the three frictions by how directly they block the north-star action.

Primary · Home

Too many steps to decide

Today's suggestions sit below a social feed. Nothing on screen says "wear this" or connects weather and plans to an outfit.

Secondary · Add items

High input effort

Every detected item asks for its own decision across five controls. Recommendation quality depends on a complete closet, so this caps everything downstream.

Solved inside Home

Difficult retrieval

Saved looks live in a month grid. I bring the right ones to Home, filtered by today's plans, instead of redesigning the calendar.

Screen 02 · Add items

Review by exception

Everything the AI detects is added by default. The user only looks at the few items the model is unsure about, and answers each with one tap. Adding twenty pieces should feel like checking two.

Business impact

How this grows the north star

A weekly retained outfit planner returns in a later week and selects or plans at least one outfit. Each design choice pulls one of three levers.

North star

Weekly retained outfit planners

Levers
Select more oftenWear this is one tap, and it counts as a selection, so intent becomes a logged outfit.

Commercial placement

Shopping that earns its place

Research flagged shopping suggestions competing with outfits. These rules keep products relevant and out of the way of the decision.

Never in the decision

No products in Today's pick, the alternates or Wear again. The first screen is only clothes she owns.

Start from a real gap

A product appears only when the closet is missing something her plans need, and the card says how many owned pieces it pairs with.

Own it first

"I have one" sits beside "Shop picks". Adding what she already owns is always the first option, which also improves the closet data.

Label and limit

Partner products are marked, behind one tap, one gap card per day, and the card can be hidden.

Validation

What I'd test first

These are hypotheses from a short brief and a single review thread. Each ships behind a flag with a holdout.

Test 1 · Home

Today's pick above the fold

Hypothesis. One explained pick with Wear this raises the share of sessions ending in a selection.

Success. Week-2 retained planners up; median time to select down; reshuffles flat.

Test 2 · Home

Plan tomorrow prompt

Hypothesis. Asking right after a selection creates the next session.

Success. Next-day return rate and planned days per week up, with no rise in notification opt-outs.

Test 3 · Add items

Added by default, checks only

Hypothesis. Opt-out review raises items added per upload without more wrong data.

Success. Items per upload and completion up; later edit and delete rate stays flat.
