Content review

Senior content strategist pass on every word in the product screen. The reader is an occasional interviewer with ten minutes, often standing at a site, sometimes running their first interview. The content has three jobs: tell Owen what's his, keep him from repeating anyone, and help him write evidence that compares with everyone else's. The team's criteria, definitions and questions are out of scope. They are theirs, and they stay verbatim.

Never put words in anyone's mouth

Quotation marks only for words someone actually wrote. Everything else is attributed plainly: “Jordan asked in their cover note…”

Facts first, Canopy's advice second

Lead with what the team and Jordan provided. Canopy's own words are limited to labels, instructions and status.

Written for ten minutes and a hard hat

The key word comes first in every line. Buttons say what happens. No line asks the reader to work out who “the panel” is.

What changed from v1

One frame for all four steps
  • Focus mode: product sidebar and permanent right panels removed
  • The same top bar, 920px column and bottom action bar in every step
  • Steps fade in place, so the page never jumps layout
The two criteria are the thread
  • Prepare: two cards hold the questions
  • Interview: each question carries its criterion tag
  • Score: one criterion per screen, then a summary pair
  • Done: the pair shows finished scores
  • Top bar pills track them throughout
Three words instead of timestamps
  • v2 had a tap to save the time of good evidence. A bare time is a weak memory cue, and nothing showed it helped, so it's gone
  • Now: as he moves on, Owen jots three words from Jordan's answer. His own words are a far stronger cue
  • Ending the interview opens Score at once, the biggest lever against forgetting
  • Paper option: a pocket card with the three questions

Rationale

words · limit 250

Owen opens Canopy ten minutes before a site visit. Today the page leads with contact details and a blank scorecard. The redesign leads with what he needs most: his hour is for two criteria.

The whole flow is one focused frame in four steps: Prepare, Interview, Score, Done. His two criteria are the thread. When he prepares, they hold the team's questions. During the visit each question carries its criterion, and as he moves on he can jot three words from Jordan's answer. A simulated field test suggested people on site won't write full notes. When the visit ends, Score opens straight away, one criterion per screen, showing each question beside his own words. At the end, the criteria show as done.

Prepare adds three plain lines: what the other interviewers already asked, as topics; the one application fact to go deeper on; and the question Jordan raised in their cover note. It reads in about two minutes, inside the five-minute guardrail. Jordan's details sit in the side rail, who asked what is laid out by interviewer, and tips sit beside each question.

The other interviewers appear only as plan and status. Ratings, recommendations and notes stay locked until Owen submits. Every word comes from the team or from Jordan's application, and the scorecard keeps all its fields.

Expected effect on the north star

North star: the share of interviews where the interviewer scores every criterion their interview covers, with written evidence, within 24 hours. Targets are hypotheses for an A/B test, not forecasts.

Rules kept

✓ Blind

Before submitting: which interviews happened, what they covered and asked, and who submitted. Nothing they concluded.

✓ Team's words

Criteria, definitions and questions are verbatim. Only their order and grouping change.

✓ Nothing generated

No recording, no transcription. Every word, from the three-word jots to the evidence, is typed by the interviewer.

✓ Scorecard fields

Rating, recommendation and all five criteria are kept. Only their order and timing change.

Assumptions: the team tags each question with a criterion and marks overlapping questions when they write the plan. Each criterion already has an evidence field.
